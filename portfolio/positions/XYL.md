## Thesis

Xylem is the largest pure-play water technology company by revenue, operating across the full water cycle: transport (pumps, mixers), treatment (filtration, UV, membrane), testing (YSI instruments), and smart infrastructure (Sensus smart meters, BlueTriton analytics software). The 2023 Evoqua acquisition doubled industrial water treatment exposure and extended recurring service revenue. Long-term demand is structurally underpinned by aging infrastructure replacement (US Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act allocates ~$55B to water, per DOT disclosures), industrial water reuse mandates, and water scarcity driving smart-network efficiency spend. Positioned at 7% as a quality industrial compounder with pricing power, expanding software/services mix, and low cyclical correlation versus the rest of the portfolio.

## Bull case

- Water infrastructure replacement cycle: EPA estimates 2.4M miles of water mains require replacement in the US over coming decades (per EPA drinking water infrastructure report); Xylem pumps and pipes are in the critical path.
- Sensus smart metering + digital: recurring SaaS-like revenue stream (Advanced Metering Infrastructure, demand analytics); utilities are multi-year contract buyers with high switching costs.
- Evoqua integration: ~$1.5B revenue added (FY2023 pro-forma, per 8-K filing); industrial water treatment (semiconductor, pharma, food & beverage) diversifies away from municipal budget cycles.
- Pricing power: essential infrastructure with long replacement cycles; water rates are regulated and consistently approved by municipalities, passing input costs through.
- International exposure (~50%+ revenue outside US, unverified): Europe's water infrastructure investment acceleration under EU Water Framework Directive and climate adaptation spending.

## Bear case & risks

- Municipal budget sensitivity: ~40–50% of revenue tied to municipal end markets (unverified); fiscal stress on city/county budgets delays large capex orders.
- Evoqua integration risk: synergy realization and margin expansion from $1.7B deal (paid ~$7.5B, unverified) is multi-year; execution missteps could weigh on FCF.
- Valuation: trades at industrial premium (~25–30x forward P/E, unverified); de-rates if infrastructure spending delays materialize.
- FX headwinds: meaningful Europe/emerging markets revenue makes earnings sensitive to USD strength.
- Competition: Grundfos (private), Veolia, Danaher's water segment, Rexnord compete across product lines; no single moat across the full portfolio.
